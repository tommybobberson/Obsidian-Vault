# Overview
**Discrete**: A [[discrete probability distribution]] for a variable X is the [[set]] of probabilities assigned to each possible variable (event) in X.

**Continuous**: Termed a [[probability density function]]. When the function,$f$, is plotted on a coordinate plane, the probability that a variable X lies between the values of a and b = **the area under the curve of the pdf function between a and b**.
$$\int_{b}^{a} f(x) dx$$
- The area under f(x) must sum to 1

**sets of variables**:
- Sets of variables have probability distributions called [[joint distributions]]
- The joint distribution of a set of variables V is the **set of probabilities of each ==possible combination of variable values in V==**
- probabilities must sum to 1
 - $P_{X,Y}​(x,y)≥0$ for all $x∈R_X​,y∈R_Y$
 - **representation of joint distributions**:
	 - tabular: tablet of all possible combinations of variable values
	 - structural causal models​
