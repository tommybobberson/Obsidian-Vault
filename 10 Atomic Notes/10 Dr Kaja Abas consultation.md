## Learnings
- [[vaccine inequity]]: 
	- **supply issues**: vaccine makers have traditionally targeted, and thus vaccine tech has often been designed for, high income countries. This translates to both pricing according to the purchasing power of these wealthier nations, and development of vaccines to address issues in high-value markets which they see will bring them value. This is what [[GAVI]] aims to tackle;
	  Companies like Moderna, Pfizer, GSK often target higher income countries while pharma and biotech companies in China, india and Africa seem to need a greater amount of time to get the same vaxxes to market - be it due to patented tech in vaccine manufacturing or falling behind.
	- **infrastructure issues**: whilst the cost of obtaining vaccines exist, the country must have sufficient healthcare infrastructure in place to distribute these effectively. the lack of [[access to healthcare]] and [[healthcare literacy]] is of particular issue to Middle and Low Income Countries (MILCs).
	- **private players**: patents exist, lack of demand and purchasing power for certain tech to tackle infrastructure issues makes it such that private, for-profit, organisations will not pursue vaccines and tech to solve issues that exist in MLIC. ==e.g. vaccine patches that exist which can be self-administered, but these are like $40 per patch, which is alot for MLICs==
	- **Lack of private players**: the vaccine dev market is oligarchic, with only a few major players. Yet, given the governments are buying vaccines enmasse, they have some sort of negotiating power to keep prices in check. e.g. if the indian govt wants to negotiate for a vaccine for all their children in a year, whichever company negotiating with them has an order of 27,000,000 vaccines a year on the line. Thus, they can't charge too much lest they risk losing a substantial order
	
- [[measuring vaccine impact]]: cases aren't as good an indicator as DALYs or Deaths as it's hard to define a case (hospitalised, severe?, unreported? etc.etc.)
- [[vaccine cost]]: other than the cost of the vaccine, which can be brought down through tech such as [[intra-dermal vaccinations]], which require a much lower dosage of the vaccine (and as such manufacturing cost) or mass manufacturing, the logistics of producing, distributing and administering the vaccines comprise a significant amount of the costs of vaccination as a strategy. (e.g. cold store, training healthcare workers to administer vaccines, continuous monitoring for side effects of the vaccines, clinical trials)

- [[the value of modelling vaccine costs and impacts]]:
	  - for current vaccines: you know what to continue and what vaccines you can slowly cut off / stop once there is no more benefit
	  - for future/new vaccines: to know if other measures might be a better expenditure as compared to vaccines
